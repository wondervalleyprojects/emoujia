# About Emoujia

## The idea

Emoujia started from a simple question Japhy Grant asked: how could someone's personal data work for them, and be pleasurable and fun?

Most personal data runs the other way. A phone keeps careful records of how you live, and those records mostly feed systems built to sell to you. The Frequently Used section of an emoji keyboard is a small, overlooked example. Nobody asked for it. Your phone builds it quietly, thirty slots ranked by habit. It might be the most honest self-portrait you own.

Emoujia takes that portrait and hands it back to you as a reading, the way a palmist reads a hand. You bring the data. A machine reads it. You get something pleasurable out of it: a ruling sign, a shadow, a forecast, an essence, and a card to share if you want. Then it lets go. The studio keeps nothing about the reading.

That is the whole experiment: your data, working for you, for the fun of it, on terms you can see.

## What it is

- A Wonder Valley Projects Lab, part of the studio's Lab face (experiments, instruments, readings).
- Live at [emoujia.com](https://emoujia.com). Register ID EMJ-001.
- Read by a machine, kept by no one. Every reading is live machine output that no person reviews, and it is labeled that way on the page and on the share card.

## The terms you can see

- **What you bring:** one screenshot of your emoji keyboard.
- **Who reads it:** Google's Gemini model. On the free tier, Google's terms let it use what it receives to improve its models. Emoujia says so under the upload button. This is a logged exception to the studio's rule that what participants share is never offered for training, taken to keep the Lab free to run (EMJ-001, 1 Nov 2026, review 1 May 2027).
- **What the studio keeps:** nothing about the reading. If you ask, the reading is emailed to you and not stored. Your email address is kept only if you check "Subscribe for more information," and one email to emoujia@wondervalleyprojects.com removes it.

## Why it lives in the Lab

Every world at Wonder Valley Projects starts in the Lab. Emoujia is a small instrument for a large question the studio cares about: what technology looks like when it serves the person using it, with consent, honesty about who did what, and some delight. It is also proof that this can be done for almost nothing.
